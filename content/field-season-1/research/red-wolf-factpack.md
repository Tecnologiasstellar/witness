# Red Wolf (Canis rufus) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-26 (research agent, web-verified). For chapter drafting; every claim sourced; conflicts flagged. USE "roughly two dozen known wild wolves" — never one exact count.

## 1. Current status
- Known wild (mid-2026): ~22 adults + 2026 pups, all in NC (USFWS via Center for Biological Diversity 2026-07-15: biologicaldiversity.org/.../four-litters-of-wild-red-wolf-pups-born-in-north-carolina-2026-07-15). May 2026: 26 known (wfae.org 2026-05-20). Count ladder: 16 known Feb 2025 → 17–19 Dec 2024 → 18 collared / 28–31 est Aug 2025 (ncwf.org) → ~30 Sept 2025 (coastalreview.org) → 26 May 2026 → 22 adults July 2026.
- Spring 2026: FOUR wild litters, 12–16 surviving pups (one litter on private Prey-for-the-Pack land).
- Captive/SAFE: ~280–284 across 52 facilities (goal 400) (reflectionriding.org update Oct 2025; ncwf.org; coastalreview.org).
- Recovery area: 1.7M acres, Dare/Hyde/Tyrrell/Washington/Beaufort counties, centered on Alligator River + Pocosin Lakes NWRs.
- fws.gov 403s automated fetch; 2025–2026 Red Wolf Release Plan exists at fws.gov/media/2025-2026-red-wolf-release-plan — verify in browser before citing as primary.

## 2. The past (threat chain)
- Ranged across the US Southeast; bounties/trapping/poison + habitat loss collapsed range to TX/LA coast; coyote hybridization as numbers fell (nwf.org Oct-Nov 2020; wildlandsnetwork.org history).
- Listed 1973. Declared EXTINCT IN THE WILD 1980; of 17 captured, 14 founders — every living red wolf descends from them (nywolf.org; redwolves.com).
- 1987: four pairs released at Alligator River NWR — first carnivore reintroduction of a species extinct in the wild; first wild litter 1988.
- Peak late-2000s ~120–130 (sources differ: 130+ in 2006 WCC; ~120 by 2012 NWF; ~120 by 2007 Coastal Review) — report as range.
- Collapse: gunshot mortality fractured packs → coyote incursion; 2015 USFWS suspends releases; 2018 proposal to shrink range; Nov 2018 court: ESA violation (awionline.org). By 2020: ~7 known/collared.

## 3. The present
- Jan 22, 2021 injunction ordered releases; 2023 settlement locks 8 years of annual release plans (awionline.org winter-2023; selc.org 403'd).
- Litters resumed 2021–22; ~16 pups/4 litters 2025; 4 litters 12–16 pups 2026.
- Vehicle strikes = leading death cause: 8 killed since 2020, nearly all US-64; June 2024 breeding male killed → 5 pups died after abandonment (defenders.org; wfae.org).
- Coyote sterilization placeholder strategy (sterilized territorial coyotes hold ground without breeding; 12 sterilized 2021–22 season) (ncwf.org/blog/red-wolf-science).
- Landowner coexistence: Prey for the Pack; Red Wolf Coalition (Columbia, NC) education.

## 4. The future (levers)
- Release pipeline: settlement-mandated annual plans; 33 SAFE breeding pairs for 2025–26.
- US-64 crossings FUNDED: $25M federal grant Dec 20, 2024 (Wildlife Crossings Pilot Program) → 11 underpasses + fencing through Alligator River NWR; ~$31M total w/ matching (coastalreview.org July 2026); "millions raised from thousands of donors" (CBD $2M vs Wildlands ~$4M — conflict). Construction ~2028–2031.
- Recovery plan (final, Sept 29, 2023): three wild populations, 740 wolves, ~50-year horizon; no new sites named yet; 2026 J. Wildlife Mgmt paper: military lands candidates (wildlife.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jwmg.70232).
- Ghost lineage: Galveston Island coyotes avg 13% red-wolf ancestry (0.9–37%), mainland TX avg 21%, red-wolf mtDNA haplotype found — potential genetic rescue reservoir for a 14-founder species (Barnes et al. 2022, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9664737).

## 5. Timeline
1973 ESA listing · 1980 extinct in wild, 14 founders · 1987 Alligator River release, first litter 1988 · late-2000s peak ~120–130 · 2015 releases suspended → ~7 by 2020 · Jan 22, 2021 court orders releases; 2023 settlement · Sept 29, 2023 recovery plan (3 pops, 740) · Dec 20, 2024 $25M crossings grant · Apr 2025 first-ever cross-foster (captive pup into wild litter; thriving) · July 2026 four litters, 12–16 pups, ~22 adults.

## 6. Known / Unknown
KNOWN: all wild red wolves live on the Albemarle Peninsula, NC — world's most endangered wolf; species descends from 14 founders; collapse 120–130 → ~7 (2020) → ~two dozen + pups; US-64 strikes leading death cause with funded fix; NASEM 2019: taxonomically valid distinct species, USFWS manages as such (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542544).
UNKNOWN: taxonomy debate live (vonHoldt genomics: closer to coyotes, possible ancient-hybrid origin; NASEM still found distinct lineage — present as live science with clear regulatory answer); true wild count (only collared are "known"); peak year/figure varies; July 2025 CBD listing lawsuit pending; crowd-raised crossings figure ($2M vs $4M).

## 7. Character details
- 45–80 lbs, ~26 in shoulder, ~4 ft; reddish fur behind ears/neck/legs; between gray wolf and coyote in size.
- Packs = a breeding pair + offspring, 5–8 — a family, not a horde.
- Howl higher-pitched than gray wolf (verify phrasing vs Red Wolf Coalition before print); public "howling" events exist.
- Cross-fostering works: Apr 2025 captive pup slipped into wild six-pup litter + three wild pups into SAFE — genes moved both ways, all thriving (ncwf.org; coastalreview.org).
- Quote — program coordinator Emily Weller: "We cannot recover this species on our own... it's the relationships, the trust, the collaboration, that really determine what's achievable." (coastalreview.org Sept 2025)
