# Wollemi Pine (Wollemia nobilis) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-27 (research agent, web-verified). THE FIRST PLANT chapter. Secrecy is a conservation tool — the chapter says so and never hints at location beyond "a remote canyon system in Wollemi National Park, ~150 km NW of Sydney."

## Current status
- Wild: ONE population, four small stands. Official counts conflict: Recovery Plan (effective 27 Mar 2025): **45 mature + 46 seedlings**; NPWS: "46 adult + 43 juvenile". Use "45–46 mature trees," both figures dated. 2020 census: 89 individuals, 46 mature. (Clonal clumps make "an individual" ambiguous.)
- IUCN Critically Endangered (B1ab(iii)+2ab(iii)); EOO <100 km², AOO <10 km² (via RBGE threatened-conifers; iucnredlist 403'd).
- Insurance population: cultivated worldwide since 2005 Sotheby's auction (292 saplings, AU$1,590,162) / 2006 retail; "I Spy a Wollemi Pine" survey documented cultivated trees in **27 countries**; global meta-collection 2024–25: 210+ young trees to botanic gardens (6-tree genetically diverse micro-populations each; BGCI/Forestry England).
- Sept 2024 (30th anniversary): translocated populations augmented +379 saplings each → **>835 translocated saplings total**; backup collection at Mt Tomah. Minister Sharpe: "the risk of losing the entire population to a single catastrophic event such as disease or wildfire is high."
- No 2026-specific update found as of 2026-08-27.

## Past (threat chain)
- Lineage ~90 Myr old: pollen indistinguishable from fossil genus *Dilwynites* (Cretaceous, Gondwana); *Dilwynites* vanishes from record ~2 Mya — presumed extinct.
- **10 Sept 1994**: David Noble, off-duty NPWS ranger, rappels into a Blue Mountains canyon and finds them. Carrick Chambers (RBG Sydney): "the equivalent of finding a small dinosaur alive on Earth" (verify exact phrasing vs outlet).
- Why so few: Australia dried over millions of years; survivors confined to sheltered fire-protected canyon refugia; genome links decline to retrotransposon burst 8–6 Mya.
- *Phytophthora cinnamomi* detected 2005 (+ P. multivora 2009) — "thought to have been introduced by unauthorised visitors" (contaminated boots/equipment). THE argument for secrecy.
- Black Summer 2019–20: Gospers Mountain fire burned ~90% of Wollemi NP; covert operation saved groves; of 49 large trees only 4 unscorched; canopy held (decisive); most small stems badly burnt, few resprouting.
- Illegal visitation: footprints/campsites found; trampling + pathogen spread → access restricted to "minimum essential personnel."

## Present
- Secrecy: location secret by NSW Government policy; even the 2020 fire rescue was kept quiet to protect it.
- Translocation: experimental site 2012 (83% seedling survival); May 2019: 436 saplings in two remote gorges — burned months later before establishing; replanted/augmented to >835 (2024). Establishment = open question.
- Genetics: essentially CLONAL — ~800 loci study found NO detectable diversity; 12.2 Gb genome (2023, PNAS/PMC10473749) confirms near-zero nucleotide diversity.
- Global cultivation: Mount Annan living collection, Mt Tomah backup, international meta-collection; public can see them at botanic gardens worldwide (NPWS name-checks Kew and Tokyo Disneyland).

## Future (levers)
- More translocation sites (recovery plan goal: increase size + geographic spread); soil-property research guides selection.
- Phytophthora hygiene: access control, decontamination, treatment.
- Fire/climate management: canopy integrity, remote irrigation, recovery-time-between-fires is the climate-era risk (ANU; original URL 404'd — find mirror).
- Public's role: BUY and grow cultivated trees (legal since 2006; sales fund conservation), report to surveys, visit gardens — and NEVER seek the wild site.

## Timeline

## Known / Unknown
KNOWN: counted almost tree-by-tree; threats identified; insurance population global; the 2020 rescue worked.
UNKNOWN: exact wild count (45 vs 46 mature — clonality); wild recruitment (burnt juveniles' resprouting, seedling survival); whether translocation sites establish (burned before establishing, replanted); canyon climate outlook; whether near-zero diversity dooms adaptability or epigenetics compensates.

## Character
- "Coco Pops" bark — dark bubbly bark "like Coco Pops welded together."
- Self-coppicing: multiple trunks like bamboo (some >20 stems); biggest tree "King Billy" ~40 m, oldest trunk ~400–450 yrs, root system possibly >1,000 yrs.
- A tree that stood among polar dinosaurs, found alive an hour's flight from Sydney.
- The 2020 drama: firefighters winched from helicopters into the gorge, daily pumping, water-bombers laying retardant on the rim — a secret mission for trees.
- Simon Duffy (Botanic Gardens of Sydney, 2024): "the poster child for conservation."
