# Amur Leopard (Panthera pardus orientalis) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-27 (research agent, web-verified). NEVER state one combined total: "about 130 in Russia (2025), 115 in China's park (2025), at least 27 crossing between them — well over 200, exact total unknown."

## Current status
- Russia: peer-reviewed SECR estimate **118 (CI 115–121)**, density 2.46/100 km², from 206 camera stations over 3,620 km², winter–spring 2022; 104 adults + 12 cubs identified (Marchenkova et al. 2025, Wildlife Letters, onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/wll2.70024). Round "~130" used by WCS/media 2025 (mongabay short-article 2025/07); park's 2023 figure 128–130.
- Core-area density 1.86/100 km² (2024 data, pub. Jun 2025) — up 183% from 0.65 in 2015 (conservewildcats.org/2025/06/24/amur-leopard-density).
- China NE Tiger & Leopard NP: **115** as of 2025, up from 42 in 2017; ≥15 cubs in past year; announced Jul 29 2026 (globaltimes.cn/page/202607/1367047.shtml). 27 leopards cross the border regularly — no simple sum.
- IUCN ~130 mature (2023); WWF-UK "fewer than 200".

## Past (threat chain)
- Pelts: 1999 undercover — poached pelts offered at $500 (F) / $1,000 (M) near Kedrovaya Pad (livescience.com/49905).
- Prey (sika/roe deer, boar) poached for meat; leopards treated as competitors.
- Fire: deliberate field burning — 46% of habitat burned ≥once 1996–2003, 12–22%/yr, converting forest to open land leopards avoid (conservewildcats Tigris fire report PDF).
- Range collapse to one SW Primorye pocket; extirpated from Korea ~1970. Lows: 38–46 (1972–73), 30–36 (1990–91), **19–26 in 2007** ("about 30") — world's rarest big cat.

## Present
- Land of the Leopard NP (Apr 2012), ~72% of remaining habitat; anti-poaching, fire suppression, prey recovery. Rybin (WCS): "Without the protected area... a very different story."
- ~300 camera traps (largest network in any Russian PA); each cat ID'd by unique rosettes. 2024: 9,000+ images, ~1,000 of leopards.
- Named cats: Typhoon (first recorded territorial call, NatGeo), Kazanova (self-dispersed to Ussuriysky), Narva (released female).
- Distemper: confirmed in wild leopard 2018 (Cornell); dogs/raccoon dogs likely source.
- Narvinsky tunnel (Mar 26, 2016): Russia's first wildlife tunnel, 575 m, buried the highway under a key corridor.
- China park (formal 2021): ~40,000 ha reforested, five cross-border corridors, AI cameras.

## Future (levers)
- Transboundary growth into China (42→115).
- Second population: 3 released into Ussuriysky Reserve May 2023 (first return in ~50 yrs; Narva doing well 2025); Lazovsky Reintroduction Centre proposed — breeding population in 10–15 yrs (Aramilev).
- Distemper: 2022 Comms Biology PVA — 10.3–99.9% 100-yr extinction risk unmitigated; low-coverage vaccination + dog management + habitat expansion helps (nature.com/articles/s42003-022-04127-9).
- Genetics: bottleneck load real (kinked tails, white paws) but unquantified; EEP captive pool 217 across 88 institutions (2018), 91% founder diversity from 14 founders (WAZA GSMP).
- Marchenkova: "We hope to see Amur leopards back across their historic range in two decades."

## Known / Unknown
KNOWN: recovery from ~30 to ~130 in Russia through pure protection (no reintroduction until the deliberate 2023 second-population project); each cat individually identified.
UNKNOWN: true transboundary total (overlap); census-method differences (park counts vs SECR — state dates+methods); wild inbreeding cost; distemper exposure rate.
UNVERIFIED (soften): "-30°C winters" → "harsh winters, deep snow, extreme cold"; "longest legs of any leopard" → "long legs adapted to deep snow".

## Character
- Thickest leopard coat: 20–25 mm summer → ~70 mm (7 cm) winter (WWF-UK). Long furry tail wrapped for warmth. Snow leopard's forest cousin in temperate forest, not jungle.
- Rosettes larger/wider-spaced than any other leopard — a fingerprint.
- Males 32–48 kg (to 75), females 25–43 kg. Whole wild world ~3,000 km² on the Russia–China border.
