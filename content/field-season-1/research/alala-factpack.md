# ʻAlalā / Hawaiian Crow (Corvus hawaiiensis) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-27 (research agent, web-verified). Extinct in the wild since 2002 — the season's extinct-in-wild chapter, and a live comeback story.

## Current status
- Extinct in the wild since 2002 (last wild pair, South Kona, Hawaiʻi Island). Sole survivor of up to five endemic Hawaiian corvids.
- Conservation care: "roughly 110–120" (DLNR 2025: 110; SDZWA Dec 2024 "more than 110"; ABC "120+"; 2026 preprint ~120). Two SDZWA facilities: Keauhou BCC (Volcano, Hawaiʻi Is.) + Maui BCC (Olinda).
- **THE MAUI RELEASE**: Nov 2024 (announced Dec 4), five juveniles (3M/2F, hatched summer 2023) released in Kīpahulu Forest Reserve, leeward Haleakalā, East Maui — first release ever OFF Hawaiʻi Island. Why Maui: NO ʻio (Hawaiian hawk) there.
- **As of Jan 2026: ALL FIVE ALIVE past one year** — foraging native plants (pūkiawe, maile, kāwaʻu, ʻōlapa), weathering storms (civilbeat.org 2026/01). Late 2025: two paired and produced AN EGG — female sat full ~21-day incubation; egg's fate unknown (first attempts rarely hatch).
- Nov 2025: two more birds staged in the field aviary for release ("flock of 7" pending). UNVERIFIED whether released as of Aug 2026 — don't claim; write around.
- Monitoring: first-ever GPS backpacks on ʻalalā + VHF + automated feeders (weaning planned).
- The five birds were named by students from five Maui schools — the naming fact is sourced, the individual names were NOT published (don't invent; "Kanileo" is a captive male, NOT one of the five).

## Past (threat chain)
- Deforestation (agriculture/grazing) of 1,000–8,000 ft forests; rats/cats/mongoose on nests; avian malaria + pox (mosquitoes); toxoplasmosis (cats); ʻio predation on naive released birds; shooting historically.
- Decline: widespread 1890s → pockets by 1950s → 76 birds in 4 areas (1976) → 13 (1991/92) → last wild pair 2002.
- 1993–98 South Kona releases: 27 captive-reared juveniles; 21 died (ʻio documented); last 6 pulled back 1998–99.
- 2016–2020 Puʻu Makaʻala releases (Hawaiʻi Is.): "about 30" released (29 vs 30 conflict); ~25 died (18 bodies; ʻio a leading cause); 5 survivors recaptured Oct 2020.

## Present
- Program from late-1970s captures + 1990s wild-egg harvests; grew from <20 (late 1990s) to ~110–120.
- Genetics: **9 founders**; one founder pair contributed ~50% of ancestry (Conservation Genetics 2015). 2026 preprint: >50% egg-hatch failure linked to recessive lethals — an honest ceiling.
- Husbandry: artificial incubation, puppet-rearing, pair management. Predator-aversion training with taxidermy ʻio; post-release, trained birds mobbed ʻio — first recorded wild mobbing by juvenile flocks (Greggor 2021; Springer 2022).
- TOOL USE: Rutz et al. Nature cover, Sep 15 2016 — 78% of 104 tested birds spontaneously used stick tools; juveniles develop it untrained; one of only TWO habitual tool-using crows on Earth (with New Caledonian crow).
- Culture: ʻaumākua (family guardians), in the Kumulipo creation chant. Keanini Aarona: "These birds, they are our ancestors." Seed disperser for 30+ native fruits (sole disperser for some) — the forest struggles to regenerate without them.

## Future (levers)
- Grow the Maui flock (Kīpahulu; ʻio-free).
- Mosquito suppression: Birds, Not Mosquitoes — Wolbachia IIT, ~500,000 male mosquitoes/week by helicopter/drone over East Maui; NPS $13.2M.
- Eventual Hawaiʻi Island return once ʻio dynamics are understood (Maui is explicitly a stepping stone).
- Genetic management against the 9-founder ceiling (pedigree pairing, genome-guided).
- NOTE: no published ʻalalā-specific predator-proof exclosure plan — don't claim one.

## Timeline
1890s widespread, decline begins · 1976 = 76 birds · late 1970s captive flock founded (9 founders) · 1993–98 Kona releases fail (21 of 27 die) · 2002 extinct in the wild · Sep 2016 Nature tool-use cover · 2016–2020 ~30 released, ~25 die, 5 recalled · Nov 2024 FIVE RELEASED ON MAUI (Kīpahulu) · late 2025 first wild egg; Nov 2025 two more staged · Jan 2026 all five alive past one year.

## Known / Unknown
KNOWN: five birds free and alive 12+ months — already better than prior cohorts; trained fear of ʻio persisted in the wild; tool use is species-wide.
UNKNOWN: long-term survival and breeding success (first egg unknown fate); whether the Nov 2025 pair is out; the 9-founder genetic ceiling (>50% hatch failure); whether wild culture survived captivity (captive vocal repertoire measurably changed — USGS; EA warns captive birds "are expected to continue to lose their wild traits").

## Character
- Voice: "dozens of different calls... sweet chirps and human-like cries to rhythmic whooping... 'the monkey call'" (Civil Beat). Counts differ (24 categories vs 34+) → use "roughly two dozen"/"dozens".
- Quote — Martin Frye (MFBRP): "In the deep past, people picked out that voice from all the voices of the forest… We can't lose that voice."
- Larger than mainland crows; bristly bill feathers; lifelong pair bonds; playful; straight beak + binocular vision suit tool use.
- Release-day scene (sourced): hesitation at the aviary door, walking the frame, then tree to tree, "vocalizing... putting their new forest neighbors on notice that they had arrived"; staff wept.
- People to quote: Keanini Aarona ("I feel almost like a parent... It does feel worth getting pooped on"), Tess Hebebrand (the egg; "We were definitely shedding some tears"), Hanna Mounce, Gavin Nuttall ("this is the home they deserve").
