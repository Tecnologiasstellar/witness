# Kākāpō (Strigops habroptila) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-26 (research agent, web-verified against DOC primary pages). Every claim sourced; conflicts flagged.

## 1. Current status
- Official population 236 as of 6 Jan 2026 (season start), incl. 83 breeding-age females (doc.govt.nz 2026 media release "kakapo-breeding-season-officially-underway"). DOC live counter shows 235 (undated) — report both.
- 2026 = BIGGEST BREEDING SEASON ON RECORD: 95+ chicks hatched by early April (prev record 73 fledglings, 2019); 10 chick deaths + 3 in vet care at that date; chicks count officially only at 150 days (mongabay short-article 2026/04). Mid-season secondary figures (~256 eggs/~80 nests/106 hatched/99 alive) UNVERIFIED vs DOC primary (RNZ 403'd).
- First chick of the season hatched Valentine's Day 2026 (DOC release). Four-year gap since 2022 season (rimu mast).
- Sites (per DOC habitat page, name only at this level): Whenua Hou/Codfish (since 1987), Pukenui/Anchor (2005), Te Kākahu/Chalky (2003, low-intervention), Sanctuary Mountain Maungatautari (mainland, fenced, Jul 2023), Coal Island/Te Puka-Hereka (male-only trial, May 2024). Breeding islands: Whenua Hou, Anchor, Chalky.
- Status: NZ "Threatened – Nationally Critical"; IUCN Critically Endangered (iucnredlist 403'd — manual check of assessment year needed before print).

## 2. The past (threat chain)
- Evolved for aerial predators: nocturnal, ground-dwelling, freeze-in-place, strong scent, flightless — all useless/deadly against introduced mammals hunting by smell (DOC behaviour page).
- Polynesian settlement: hunting + rats + dogs; European: forest clearance + cats, stoats, more rats (DOC history page).
- 1894: conservationist Richard Henry moved hundreds to Resolution Island; stoats swam over within six years — first great NZ conservation heartbreak (DOC history).
- 1949–73: 60+ expeditions found six males; by 1977, 18 more males, NO females. 1977: ~200 found on Rakiura/Stewart Island; females confirmed 1980; cat predation → all birds evacuated to offshore islands 1980–1997.
- 1995 LOW POINT: 51 birds (31 males, 20 females); Recovery Programme founded (DOC + Ngāi Tahu).
- Richard Henry THE BIRD: last Fiordland kākāpō (found 1975, named for the man), only Fiordland genes in the flock — fathered Kuia, Gulliver, Sinbad with Flossie in 1998; died 2010 aged ~80 (estimated). Genomic caveat: carried more harmful mutations than Stewart founders (sciencedirect S2666979X21000021).

## 3. The present
- EVERY BIRD IS NAMED, transmittered ("smart backpacks"; female units have egg-timer nesting flags), with personal tag-activated weighing feeding stations (DOC technology page).
- Breeding only in rimu mast years (every 2–4). Only lek-breeding parrot.
- AI program incl. 2019 "spermcopter" drone carrying semen across Whenua Hou — a conservation first (PLOS One 2025 journals.plos.org 0322276; stuff.co.nz).
- 2019 aspergillosis outbreak: 21 birds (~10% of species) affected, 9 died; traced to a single fungal strain (DOC outbreak page; PMC9668684).
- Mainland return: Jul 2023 four males to Maungatautari — first mainland kākāpō in ~40 years; +6 Sept 2023. They climbed the 47 km pest fence repeatedly; 7 of 10 returned south (4 → Coal Island trial May 2024); 3 stayed, good 2024–25 summer with mainland BOOMING recorded (DOC releases 2023–2025).
- 2026 strategy shift: deliberately lower-intervention ("thriving, not just surviving" — Deidre Vercoe, DOC). Partner Ngāi Tahu; sponsor Meridian Energy since 2016.

## 4. The future (levers)
- Island capacity is the binding constraint; ultimate DOC objective: unmanaged mainland populations.
- Predator Free Rakiura: ~175,000 ha — would be world's largest inhabited predator-free island, could bring kākāpō home; 6,500 ha trial run; larger operation considered for 2026 (doc.govt.nz predator-free-rakiura; predatorfreerakiura.org.nz).
- Kākāpō125+: genome of essentially every living bird sequenced (2018; ~169-genome analysis Nature Ecol Evol 2023 s41559-023-02165-y) — predicts growth, disease risk, fertility; guides pairings.
- Risks: disease (one strain hit 10% at once), stubborn infertility/early embryo death (why remains open), single predator incursion, rimu unpredictability.

## 6. Known / Unknown
KNOWN: counts are true individual censuses (every bird named/tracked) — 236/235; rimu-mast breeding; threat chain (cats/stoats/rats + hunting + clearance); all genomes sequenced.
UNKNOWN: lifespan (DOC "possibly longest-lived bird, 60–90 yrs" vs NZ Birds Online ">50, unknown" — report the uncertainty); why infertility stays high; whether 175,000 ha eradication is achievable (never done); final 2026 fledgling total (150-day rule — avoid a hard post-season number).

## 7. Character details
- The BOOM: male digs a bowl, inflates like a feathered balloon, booms every 1–2 s for up to 8 hours a night, 2–3 months straight; carries hundreds of metres (DOC; "up to 5 km" is secondary — cautious). Metallic "ching" calls between.
- Musty-sweet smell (freesia/violin-case comparisons are secondary — attribute loosely); the scent that betrays them.
- World's heaviest parrot (males to 4 kg); flightless but climbs 20 m rimu and "parachutes" down; freezes when disturbed.
- Every bird named: Sirocco, Solstice, Hoki, Elwin, Māhutonga, Motupōhue…
- SIROCCO (hatched 23 Mar 1997): hand-reared, human-imprinted; 2009 BBC fame (attempted to mate with Mark Carwardine's head, Stephen Fry filming, 30M+ views); appointed Official Spokesbird for Conservation by PM John Key 2010 (DOC sirocco page).
- Solstice: last kākāpō ever found on Rakiura (21 June 1997, winter solstice); died 2025.
- Mothers raise chicks alone, feeding up to six months after fledging.
