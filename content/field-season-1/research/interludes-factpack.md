## A — The Price of Parts (wildlife trade)

- Global illegal wildlife trade value: estimates disagree. UNODC/UNEP-INTERPOL range **US$7–23B/yr** (2016 report; earlier UNODC 2014 said $8–10B). World Bank's "$1–2 trillion" bundles illegal logging/fishing AND unpriced ecosystem losses — not comparable. **UNODC World Wildlife Crime Report 2024 deliberately names no dollar figure**; documents seizures across 162 countries/territories, ~4,000 species (2015–2021). Essay framing: "single-digit billions to over twenty billion; the UN's own 2024 report avoids one number."
- UNODC 2024's verdict: after two decades of concerted action, wildlife trafficking "has not been substantially reduced." (Strongest honest sentence available.)
- Totoaba: CITES Appendix I since 1976/77. Bladder end-market price **~$20,000–80,000+/kg** (UCSB 2025; Global Initiative $20k–81k/kg); fishers paid a small fraction. Anchor: China Dec 2018 seizure, 444 kg ≈ $26M (~$59k/kg).
- Rhino horn: keratin (same protein as fingernails) — TRAFFIC/Save the Rhino. Price honesty: cited retail "up to $65,000/kg" (2012–14 Vietnam peak) vs **WJC 2022 measured wholesale: under one-third of that, as low as one-tenth at source** (highest recorded $7,529/kg, South Africa, Feb 2022). WJC 2022: demand now driven more by luxury carvings/status than medicine. Markets: Vietnam, China.
- Enforcement wins: **Teo Boon Ching** sentenced 18 months, US federal court (SDNY), 19 Sep 2023, ~219 kg horn ≈ $2.1M (note: 18 months for 219 kg is itself a data point). **China's first totoaba convictions, Shanghai, Dec 2018**: 7 and 8 years. **CITES suspended all wildlife trade with Mexico late Mar 2023** over vaquita/totoaba failures; lifted 13 Apr 2023 after Mexico filed a compliance plan (procedural win, not nets out of water). WJC 2024: supported 84 arrests.
- Limits (published): enforcement without livelihood alternatives punishes the poorest link (Duffy et al., Conservation Biology); ban-vs-regulated-legal-trade genuinely unresolved (Biological Conservation 2021); totoaba banned since the 1970s and the vaquita still fell to ~10 — high prices make enforcement a leaky dam; farmed-totoaba supply experiments exist for this reason (UCSB 2025).
- Doors: **Wildlife Justice Commission** https://wildlifejustice.org/ (undercover intelligence-led investigations → traffickers/brokers before judges; active Jun 2026). **WildAid** https://wildaid.org/ (demand-reduction media campaigns in consumer countries; active Aug 2026; caution — rhino/totoaba not current front-page campaigns, keep sentence generic). Alternates verified: TRAFFIC traffic.org, EIA eia-international.org (has dedicated vaquita/totoaba campaign).

## B — The Uninvited (invasive species)

- IPBES Invasive Alien Species Assessment (approved Aug–Sep 2023, 86 experts, 49 countries): invasive alien species played a **major role in 60% of recorded extinctions** (of those with known drivers — precision matters), **sole driver in 16%**; global cost **>$423B/yr (2019)**, at least **quadrupling every decade since 1970**.
- Predator Free 2050 (NZ, since 2016): goal to eradicate rats, possums, mustelids nationwide. Milestone: **Miramar Peninsula, Wellington — rats, stoats, weasels eliminated (announced Nov 2023)**; wild kiwi back near the capital (Capital Kiwi; DOC blog Sep 2025). Honest wrinkle: **Predator Free 2050 Ltd (Crown funding company) disestablished 2025** as cost-cutting; work absorbed into DOC (1News, 26 May 2025). Feasibility literature says landscape-scale, fence-free eradication needs a breakthrough that does not yet exist (J. Royal Soc. NZ 2019; Biological Conservation 2023). Islands/peninsulas are where eradication reliably works.
- Birds, Not Mosquitoes (Hawaiʻi IIT — incompatible males, eggs don't hatch): **Maui releases since Nov 2023** (40M+ males by mid-2025, Smithsonian); **Kauaʻi first release 13 Feb 2025**, Alakaʻi Plateau, scaling toward ~500k/week. Flagship beneficiaries are honeycreepers — same avian-malaria system that constrains ʻalalā recovery, but do NOT call it an ʻalalā program.
- Wollemi pine: *Phytophthora cinnamomi* — an oomycete (water mould), **not a fungus** — confirmed in soil and at least one wild tree in 2005, likely on trespassers' boots (ABC Science 2005); NSW NPWS runs an active anti-Phytophthora program + Wollemi Pine Conservation Project (quarantine, sterilized footwear, secrecy). *P. multivora* also detected (2015 paper). Safe claim: "documented infection, ongoing declared threat" — NOT "the stand is dying of it."
- Xochimilco: refuge canals separated by **tezontle (volcanic rock) biofilters + gates carp/tilapia cannot pass**, plus removal fishing (Mongabay May 2024). Consistent with ch8.
- Limits (published): predator-control ethics debate is real (J. Ag. & Env. Ethics 2019 "Is it a Just War?"; compassionate-conservation critiques of 1080; counterview: eradication ends suffering permanently); continental eradication of established vertebrates essentially never achieved.
- Doors: **Island Conservation** https://www.islandconservation.org/ (removes invasives from islands, restores natives; 501(c)(3); active 2026). **Predator Free NZ Trust** https://predatorfreenz.org/ (funds/connects community trapping groups; active). Alternate: ABC via birdsnotmosquitoes.org (coalition site has no donate button — route via ABC; ABC already a ch5 door, so not repeated).
