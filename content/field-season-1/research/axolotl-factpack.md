# Axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) — Fact Pack
Compiled 2026-08-27 (research agent, web-verified; Mexican primary sources). Season symmetry: opens with vaquita (Mexico's sea), closes with axolotl (Mexico's lake).

## Current status
- Wild only in Lake Xochimilco's canals (last remnant of the Valley of Mexico's five lakes).
- Census crash (UNAM/Zambrano): ~6,000/km² (1998) → ~1,000 (2003) → ~100 (2008) → **~36/km² (2014)** — 99% in 16 years (use "about 36" per Gaceta UNAM).
- NEW CENSUS (first in a decade): UNAM Instituto de Biología + Conservation International, 115 sites across 2,500 ha; phase 1 Sep–Oct 2024, phase 2 Jan–Feb 2025; **nets caught ZERO axolotls, but eDNA detected them** — present, invisible. Full results expected 2026, "could be lower than previous counts" (gaceta.unam.mx levantan-nuevo-censo; conservation.org axolotls-are-on-the-brink).
- IUCN CR since 2006; 2019 assessment 50–1,000 mature (iucnredlist 403'd — manual check). Oct 2024 UNAM warning: could disappear from the canals without intervention.
- PARADOX: "by some estimates a million or more" in captivity (labs/pets/zoos) while nearly extinct wild.
- **2025 RELEASE STUDY (verified)**: Ramos, Mena, Schneider & Zambrano, PLOS One, Apr 30 2025 (journals.plos.org 0314257): 18 captive-bred axolotls, VHF transmitters — 10 into a restored chinampa refuge (Xochimilco), 8 into La Cantera Oriente artificial wetland. ALL 18 survived monitoring; 3 recaptured had GAINED weight (one +18.34 g) — hunting successfully. Two later killed by great egrets. Ramos: "The amazing news is that they all survived... they're hunting."

## Past (threat chain)
- Aztec: named for Xolotl, twin of Quetzalcoatl, who became an axolotl to escape sacrifice — the god who refused to die. Sold roasted in Tenochtitlan's markets; Florentine Codex: "comida de los señores".
- Thrived in five lakes (Chalco, Xochimilco, Texcoco, Zumpango, Xaltocan). Spanish drainage from 1521 onward, centuries of it; Chalco fully drained; by the 1980s the canals ran on treated wastewater.
- 1970s–80s: carp + tilapia introduced (govt food projects) — eat eggs and juveniles.
- Pollution (sewage, agrochemicals) + urbanization → the 99% crash.
- 1863: 34 axolotls shipped to Paris; six reach Duméril — founding the lab lineage behind most captive axolotls (oldest self-sustaining lab population; jstor daily race-to-save).

## Present
- UNAM Laboratorio de Restauración Ecológica (Luis Zambrano) — census, refuges, assurance colony.
- Chinampa-refuge model: canal sections beside chinampas sealed with biofilters (wood/gravel/native plants) that clean water and block carp/tilapia. Counts differ: 21 refuges (Conservation Intl 2025) vs 36 refuges + ~5.5 km canals (Fundación UNAM AdoptAxolotl 2025-26) — cite both dated. Refuges cover only ~0.5% of the network. Side benefits measured: better water, fewer invasives, higher crop yields, native fish return.
- Biology: neoteny (larval forever, breeds without metamorphosis); regenerates limbs, tail, spinal cord, heart, jaw, skin, parts of brain, scar-free. Genome 32 Gb (~10× human), Nature Feb 2018 (Nowoshilow et al.).
- Culture: on the 50-peso note since Oct 28 2021 (IBNS Banknote of the Year 2021; ~12.9M people hoard it); note modeled on "La Gorda" (Axolotitlán museum). AdoptAxolotl campaign (4th ed. 2025-26) funds colony+refuges. "Voted Mexico's favorite animal" UNVERIFIED → use "Mexico's most beloved amphibian."

## Future (levers)
- Scale refuges from 0.5% toward a connected system; restored chinampería could supply up to a quarter of the city's flowers/vegetables (Conservation Intl).
- Water: biofilters + better treatment of the wastewater feeding the canals.
- Invasive fish control (barriers + removal).
- 2025 evidence: reintroduction biologically feasible; study recommends predator-awareness training (egrets).
- Zambrano: "If we can restore this habitat and restore the axolotl's population in a city of more than 20 million people... we have hope for humanity."
- Saving Xochimilco saves more: 200+ bird species (flyway stopover), flood control, UNESCO WH (1987), Ramsar (2004).

## Timeline
Aztec era markets/Xolotl · 1521+ draining begins · 1863 Paris shipment · 1970s–80s Chalco drained; carp/tilapia; wastewater · 1987 UNESCO · 1998–2014 crash 6,000→36/km² · 2006 IUCN CR · Feb 2018 genome · Oct 28 2021 50-peso note · Sep 2024–2026 census (nets 0, eDNA yes; results 2026) · Apr 30 2025 all 18 released axolotls survived.

## Known / Unknown
KNOWN: captive-bred can survive and hunt in restored habitat; the chinampa-refuge model works and pays farmers; the animal persists (eDNA).
UNKNOWN: true wild number (last count ~36/km² 2014; "fewer than 1,000" is estimate not count); whether 1863-lineage captives fit the wild (predator training, genetics open); Xochimilco's water future in a 20M+ megacity.
Zambrano: "It is important what people say — yes, they have seen them — but we must be sure of their existence in the wild."

## Character
- Permanent Mona Lisa smile; three pairs of external feathery gills like a headdress; walks on lake bottoms — "Mexican walking fish" that is not a fish.
- IMPORTANT VISUAL: wild axolotls are DARK mottled brown-black-olive; the pink-white icon is a captive lab morph (banknote's pale axolotl = irony, modeled on museum's La Gorda).
- Chinampas: 1,000-year-old "floating gardens," still farmed — the refuge is the Aztecs' own farm system revived.
- The myth rhyme: the god who refused to die → the species refusing, so far, to go extinct.
